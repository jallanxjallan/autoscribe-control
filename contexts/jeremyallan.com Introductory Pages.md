The source will normally consist of bulleted notes written by the author in approximately the same voice as the intended final text. Treat those notes as authoritative source material.

Write in the first person unless the source clearly requires otherwise.

Do not invent facts, chronology, motives, achievements, interpretations, quotations, or connective biographical detail. Do not fill apparent gaps from general knowledge. Where the notes do not support a statement, omit it.

Preserve specific names, places, publications, book titles, dates, incidents, and other concrete details supplied in the source.

Convert bullets into natural continuous prose rather than mechanically turning each bullet into a sentence. Combine related material, change the order where this improves the narrative, and remove repetition.

The finished page should sound as though the author wrote it directly as prose. It should not sound like a biography written about him, a résumé converted into paragraphs, a publisher's author profile, or AI-generated website copy.

Use headings only when the amount or structure of the material genuinely requires them. Do not impose sections merely for symmetry.

Keep the prose economical. Do not add introductory throat-clearing, summary conclusions, calls to action, slogans, or statements telling the reader what to think about the author's work.

Links, images, navigation, metadata, and other Hugo concerns are outside the prose task unless explicitly present in the source or requested separately.

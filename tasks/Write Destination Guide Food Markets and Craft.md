Write a sensory but socially grounded passage about food, kitchens, warungs, markets, agricultural practice, artisanship, making, labour or buying.

Show HOW something is made, tasted, exchanged or learned when those process details are supported by the source. Link technique to people, materials, livelihoods, histories and relationships without reducing craft workers or cooks to attractions. Specific spices, ingredients, techniques, prices, provenance and personal encounters must not be invented. Keep the language controlled: particular textures and contrasts are better than overwrought descriptions of aroma, vibrancy or authenticity. Do not claim to have sampled food or watched its preparation unless that experience appears in the source.

For editorial notes, develop their stated central angle with plausible general explanation while avoiding invented on-site scenes. For developed passages, retain the author's details and proportions. Avoid making lists of establishments, dishes to order, market opening schedules or shopping recommendations; purchasing ethics may be discussed as enduring principles, not named vendors.

Examples: gudeg, bakmi, sambal, a neighbourhood warung, Bumi Langit's food chain, batik, silver, clay, fibre and the visitor's relationship with makers. Output one self-contained narrative passage, without title or editorial apparatus.
